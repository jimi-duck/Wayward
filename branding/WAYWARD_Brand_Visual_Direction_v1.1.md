# WAYWARD - Brand & Visual Direction

**Version 1.1 - Visual Canon Update**

## Working definition

**WAYWARD** is a fugitive survival game in a dying frontier.

The visual identity is **industrial cyberpunk rather than generic neon cyberpunk**: advanced technology exists everywhere, but it is old, regulated, patched, repurposed and controlled by institutions.

The latest concept development has clarified the visual target. WAYWARD should feel like a stolen industrial machine moving through a cyberpunk frontier that is slowly becoming stranger. The player should read the ship first as a **freighter / ore hauler**, then as a survival vehicle, and only lastly as a combat ship.

---

# 1. Brand essence

**WAYWARD**

**A fugitive survival game in a dying frontier.**

The visual identity should communicate:

**Industrial - Oppressive - Scrappy - Mysterious - Human - Dangerous**

The player should feel that they are operating a piece of working equipment, not piloting a pristine science-fiction spacecraft.

The technology is advanced, but the world is exhausted.

Everything has a previous owner.
Everything carries a warning label.
Everything is being repaired.
Everything has a serial number.

**Core visual idea:** advanced systems living inside exhausted infrastructure.

---

# 2. The three visual worlds

WAYWARD sits between three influences.

## INDUSTRIAL

Mining equipment, freight machinery, warning labels, maintenance manuals, shipping containers, electrical panels, military hardware, refinery infrastructure.

Think:

**ore hauler + industrial control room + old military equipment**

rather than:

**NASA + luxury spaceship + sleek holographic interface**

## CYBERPUNK

The cyberpunk element comes from information, surveillance and corporate/state control.

The world is full of:

**IDs - registries - checkpoints - tracking - signals - tariffs - sanctions - restricted areas - corporate branding - propaganda**

Cyberpunk should therefore exist in the systems and society, not just through neon.

Neon is allowed as a **localized environmental accent** in dense human settlements, ports and nightlife districts. It is not the default colour language of the game.

## DYSTOPIAN FRONTIER

The Scatter should feel like civilisation has been stretched too thin.

Ports are overcrowded.
Ships are patched together.
Mining installations are gigantic but exhausted.
Infrastructure is functional rather than beautiful.

The further away from civilisation the player travels, the stranger and less controlled everything becomes.

---

# 3. The most important rule: technology should look USED

Avoid the feeling:

> "This was designed for a video game."

Aim for:

> "Someone had to keep this thing running for another 14 years."

Surfaces should have:

- scratches
- dirt
- faded paint
- replacement panels
- exposed fasteners
- cable runs
- warning stickers
- serial numbers
- inspection markings
- maintenance labels
- mismatched components

Even new technology should feel like it belongs in an industrial ecosystem.

The same rule applies to interfaces: screens should feel **operational**, not decorative.

---

# 4. Core colour palette

The yellow/orange ship is the key visual anchor, but the palette must remain disciplined.

| Colour | Purpose | Approx. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Void Black** | Backgrounds, space, major UI fields | `#080D0E` |
| **Industrial White** | Primary text, ship markings | `#D8D5C6` |
| **Wayward Amber** | Brand / ship identification / interaction | `#D99A27` |
| **Signal Teal** | Navigation / known information / operational systems | `#39AEB7` |
| **Alert Red** | Continuance / danger / pursuit / failure | `#C94A3F` |
| **Dust Grey** | Secondary UI / inactive elements | `#596160` |
| **Hazard Green** | Mining / resource / industrial systems | `#748841` |
| **Deep Brown** | Rust / worn machinery / environmental detail | `#4A3A27` |

## Colour ratio

The game should be overwhelmingly dark and desaturated.

**65% black / dark grey**
**20% off-white / grey**
**8% amber**
**5% teal**
**2% red**

**Red should mean something.** It should not be decorative.

---

# 5. Wayward Amber

The amber/yellow of the ship is the strongest visual signature.

It should feel like:

**construction equipment + hazard paint + old industrial machinery**

rather than racing yellow or futuristic gold.

Use it for:

- Wayward hull panels
- player-owned equipment
- interactable objects
- mining equipment
- selected UI states
- important resources
- player identification

The ship should generally combine amber paint with **charcoal / graphite structural areas** and **off-white markings**, rather than becoming a solid yellow object.

---

# 6. Teal is information

Teal is the opposite of red.

It represents things that are:

**known - detected - operational - navigational - informational**

Use teal for:

- radar
- navigation
- scan information
- ship systems
- target information
- mining information
- route indicators
- friendly/neutral contacts

Do **not** use teal as general decoration.

---

# 7. Red is the threat

Red belongs primarily to:

**THE CONTINUANCE**

Continuance UI should have a distinct visual identity:

**Red - black - white**

Hard geometric symbols.
Authority.
Warning triangles.
Large identification numbers.
Brutalist typography.
Official language.

Examples:

**CONTINUANCE**
**NOTICE OF VIOLATION**
**VESSEL FLAGGED**
**CHECKPOINT ACTIVE**
**PURSUIT PROTOCOL**
**SURRENDER REQUIRED**

The Continuance should visually feel like **bureaucracy weaponised**.

---

# 8. Typography

Typography should feel like something that exists on machinery.

## Display

Use a wide, heavy, geometric / condensed industrial grotesque.

The WAYWARD logo should feel like:

**shipping crate stencil + government department + industrial manufacturer**

rather than a typical gaming logo.

## Interface

Use a clean condensed sans-serif.

Prefer:

- uppercase
- tight spacing
- compact labels
- numerical information
- short phrases

Examples:

**HULL 78%**
**FUEL 41%**
**KRAIL 2.3t**
**SIGNAL DETECTED**
**CHECKPOINT 1.2 AU**

## Data

Use monospaced typography for:

- serial numbers
- coordinates
- ship IDs
- timestamps
- cargo IDs
- system information

The UI should look assembled from actual equipment rather than designed as a decorative overlay.

---

# 9. WAYWARD logo

The logo should be:

**bold, horizontal, industrial and imperfect.**

Avoid:

- futuristic cursive
- glossy chrome
- excessive sci-fi bevels
- spaceship silhouettes inside the wordmark
- elaborate logo puzzles

The word itself is the identity.

Supporting terminology can appear beneath it:

**OBEDIENCE-CLASS / ORE HAULER**
**SERIAL 0B-7731-A**

The logo should be capable of appearing on:

- hull panels
- menus
- ship documentation
- loading screens
- stickers
- advertisements
- faction documents
- UI headers

# 10. Ship design language - updated from the current concepts

The latest visual exploration establishes a more specific Wayward silhouette.

The ship is **not** a boxy tug, not a fighter, and not a military gunship.

It is a **long, low, aerodynamic industrial hauler** whose geometry has been shaped by cargo, propulsion, repair and modular refits.

## Silhouette priorities

The silhouette should communicate, in this order:

**freighter -> industrial machine -> modular survival vehicle -> combat-capable ship**

### Shape language

- Long horizontal proportions
- Strong, slightly wedge-shaped forward prow
- Recessed / integrated cockpit rather than a prominent glass bubble
- A clear central hull spine
- Layered upper hull plating
- Separated side-mounted propulsion pods
- Modular side / lower utility structures
- Exposed mechanical interfaces and hardpoints
- Slightly asymmetrical or mismatched service modules where useful
- Low visual centre of mass

### Engine rule

**Do not use a large central rear engine as the main silhouette feature.**

The latest concepts work better when propulsion is split into **side / rear engine units** that visually sit outside the main cargo body. This keeps the centre of the ship readable as a freight machine and makes the silhouette more distinctive.

Engine glow should be one of the ship's few strong points of saturated light.

### Cyberpunk influence

The ship should be sleeker and stranger than a conventional industrial spacecraft, but never luxury-sleek.

The reference point is:

**cyberpunk freighter + mining hauler + patched commercial transport**

not:

**fighter jet + military carrier + polished yacht**

### Combat

Weapons should look added later.

They should read as equipment fitted to a working vessel rather than an original weapons platform.

The player has not bought a badass combat ship.

They have **turned a working machine into a vehicle for survival**.

---

# 11. Ship markings and physical language

Use persistent physical markings throughout the game.

Examples:

**WAYWARD**
**OBEDIENCE CLASS**
**0B-7731-A**

Supporting markings:

**KRAIL HANDLING**
**MAX LOAD**
**FIELD REFIT**
**AUTHORIZED USE**
**HAZARD**
**PRESSURE**
**SERVICE PORT**
**NO LIFT**
**DOCKING SIDE**

The ship should feel like a truck, mining vessel or heavy utility transport translated into space.

### Surface hierarchy

1. Painted hull panels
2. Dark mechanical structure
3. Service doors and access plates
4. Warning labels / serials
5. Exposed cables and fittings
6. Replacement parts in slightly mismatched finishes

The ship should look maintained because somebody had to maintain it - but never cosmetically restored.

---

# 12. Gameplay UI direction - current canon

The latest gameplay concepts are **calmer and more spacious** than the early HUD explorations.

The world should have enough empty space that the player can read the ship, the asteroid field and the destination without the interface fighting for attention.

## Core gameplay view

Camera:

**Third-person, slightly behind and above the Wayward.**

The ship should occupy roughly the lower-middle of the frame without filling the entire screen.

Behind it:

**asteroids - mining structures - stations - debris - distant ships - signal sources**

HUD elements sit around the scene rather than across the centre of it.

### The six functional zones

**1. Upper left - CURRENT OBJECTIVE**

Short mission steps only.

Example:

**EXTRACT KRAIL**
**> LOCATE VEIN (M-372)**
**> 1.2 KM**
**> RETURN TO WAYWARD**

**2. Upper centre - TARGET / THREAT**

Only the most important current target.

Example:

**CONTINUANCE PATROL**
**2.8 AU**
**THREAT: HIGH**

**3. Upper right - SHIP STATUS**

**HULL**
**FUEL**
**ENERGY**
**KRAIL**
**MISSILES / MUNITIONS**

The player should read the condition of the ship at a glance.

**4. Lower left - RADAR + CONTACTS**

Circular scanner paired with a short contact list.

Show:

**CONTACT**
**DISTANCE**
**TYPE**
**THREAT / STATUS**

**5. Right side - OPERATING MODE**

Examples:

**MINING MODE**
**SCAN MODE**
**COMBAT MODE**
**SILENT RUN**

Then expose only the actions relevant to that mode.

**6. Centre - WORLD**

Keep the centre comparatively clean.

The player should primarily see:

**the ship + the thing they are trying to reach + the thing they are worried about**

### Important rule: avoid HUD noise

Do not display every available piece of information simultaneously.

The HUD should answer:

**Where am I?**
**What am I doing?**
**What is threatening me?**
**What condition is my ship in?**

Anything else can move into dedicated screens.

---

# 13. Bottom-right operating mode

This area communicates the current ship mode.

Examples:

**MINING MODE**
**COMBAT MODE**
**SCAN MODE**
**SILENT RUN**

Example actions:

**[LMB] EXTRACT**
**[RMB] SCAN**
**[Q] SIGNAL**
**[E] CYCLE MODE**

The interface should feel like the player is operating a machine, not navigating a menu.

---

# 14. Communications UI

Comms should feel like **receiving a transmission**, not opening a traditional RPG dialogue box.

Example:

**INCOMING TRANSMISSION**

**TERRAN UNION CHECKPOINT**

> Vessel ID does not match registry.

**01 - RUN IT**
**02 - SPOOF REGISTRATION**
**03 - PAY INSPECTION**
**04 - TALK**
**05 - FIGHT**

### Comms rules

- Keep the message short.
- Make the situation legible immediately.
- Put consequences next to options when known.
- Use colour to communicate risk, not emotion.
- Let fitted systems, prior actions, contacts and reputation change the available options.

The player should feel like they are making an operational decision under pressure.

---

# 15. UI language

Use short, functional language.

Instead of:

> "You have encountered a patrol ship belonging to the Continuance."

Use:

**CONTINUANCE PATROL**

Instead of:

> "Your ship is running low on fuel."

Use:

**FUEL CRITICAL**

Instead of:

> "The asteroid appears to contain a valuable mineral."

Use:

**KRAIL TRACE DETECTED**

The language should feel like equipment reporting a condition.

---

# 16. Faction branding

Every major faction should look like a real institution or culture, not simply a colour variant.

## THE CONTINUANCE

**Red / black / white**

Institutional.
Governmental.
Strict.
Highly standardised.

Graphics should look mass-produced, stamped and regulated.

## MINING / INDUSTRIAL COMPANIES

**Amber / cream / dark grey**

Heavy machinery.
Shipping.
Industrial logos.
Barcode systems.
Warning symbols.

## FRONTIER INDEPENDENTS

More inconsistent.

Hand-painted marks.
Modified equipment.
Different colours.
Old logos painted over.
Home-made repairs.

## THREN

Completely different visual language.

Their graphics should not look like another human faction.

Avoid simply giving them alien text.

Instead use:

**geometry - asymmetry - biological shapes - unfamiliar repetition - strange light**

Their technology should feel difficult to understand.

The player should not immediately think:

**"Oh, that's an alien spaceship."**

The reaction should be:

**"What the hell am I looking at?"**

---

# 17. Environment

## Human space

Use **industrial density**.

Stations should look enormous, dirty and functional.

Mining rigs.
Fuel depots.
Docking arms.
Cargo lifts.
Communications towers.
Maintenance gantries.
Refinery structures.

Ships should constantly be moving through these spaces.

The frontier should feel busy because everyone is trying to survive economically.

## Deeper Scatter

Gradually remove human order.

Less traffic.
Less signage.
More abandoned machinery.
Strange signals.
Broken infrastructure.
Asteroids.
Impossible structures.
Unknown phenomena.

The visual progression should be:

**Civilisation -> decay -> isolation -> uncertainty -> alien**

---

# 18. Lighting

The basic recipe is:

**dark space + hard industrial light + selective colour**

Use:

- amber work lights
- cold teal equipment lights
- red warning lights
- white moon / starlight
- occasional sodium-orange industrial lighting

### Neon rule

Neon is a **location accent**, not the game's global lighting language.

Use stronger cyan / magenta / electric colour in:

- dense city ports
- commercial districts
- nightlife
- propaganda signage
- corporate spaces

Keep mining space and frontier gameplay comparatively restrained.

A single red beacon in a dark mining facility is more powerful than twenty neon signs.

---

# 19. Visual effects

Effects should feel physical.

Prefer:

- sparks
- exhaust
- dust
- electrical arcs
- warning lights
- impact flashes
- debris
- engine glow
- particulate clouds

Avoid excessive:

- holographic particles
- magic-looking shields
- giant lens flares
- rainbow energy explosions

WAYWARD should feel closer to **machinery breaking** than a space opera.

---

# 20. Graphic design language

The brand borrows heavily from:

**industrial manuals + freight labels + government forms + military documentation + mining equipment + old computer interfaces**

Use:

- boxes
- registration numbers
- barcode strips
- technical diagrams
- stamps
- warning triangles
- serial numbers
- inspection marks
- underlines
- measurement ticks
- schematic arrows
- coordinate grids

This creates a coherent universe without requiring constant decoration.

---

# 21. Image composition

For promotional images and moodboards, use this hierarchy:

### 1. SHIP

The Wayward is the hero.

### 2. WORLD

Asteroids, industrial stations, mining operations, damaged infrastructure.

### 3. UI

Gameplay information frames the scene.

### 4. THREAT

Continuance ships, warning signals, distant patrols.

### 5. MYSTERY

Something strange in the distance.

## Composition rule from the latest gameplay concepts

Do not overfill the frame.

The ship should have room around it.

The HUD should be legible without touching every edge of the screen.

The background should supply scale and atmosphere, not compete with the player vehicle.

The central focal triangle should usually be:

**WAYWARD -> destination -> threat**

---

# 22. What WAYWARD should NOT look like

## Do not become generic neon cyberpunk

No endless pink/purple cityscape as the default visual language.

## Do not become military sci-fi

The Wayward is not a battleship.

## Do not become sleek luxury sci-fi

The ship should never look factory-fresh or expensive.

## Do not become pure horror

The frontier contains fear and mystery, but also commerce, work and ordinary survival.

## Do not make everything futuristic

Old technology is one of the strongest aesthetic tools in the world.

## Do not make the ship boxy for the sake of “industrial”

Industrial does not have to mean square.

The current visual target is **long, low, layered and aerodynamic**, with industrial hardware attached to that form.

## Do not make the HUD noisy

Information should be prioritised, not piled on.

---

# 23. The visual test

Every asset should pass this question:

> **Could this plausibly have existed for twenty years, been used by fifty different people, repaired six times and still be operating?**

If yes, it belongs in WAYWARD.

A second test now applies to ships:

> **Does the silhouette still read clearly when the colour and texture are removed?**

If no, the ship design is relying too much on surface detail.

A third test applies to UI:

> **Can the player understand the immediate situation in two seconds?**

If no, the HUD is doing too much.

# 25. Current concept canon - based on the generated visual exploration

The following decisions have emerged from the latest concept images and should now be treated as the working visual canon.

## The Wayward ship

- Long and low rather than boxy.
- Strong forward prow.
- Central hull spine.
- Side-mounted / rear engine pods.
- No large central rear engine dominating the silhouette.
- Layered industrial plating.
- Yellow / orange painted panels over dark mechanical structure.
- Blue-cyan engine glow.
- Worn surfaces and exposed service hardware.
- Modular hardpoints.
- Combat capability visibly secondary to freight / mining function.

## Gameplay view

- Calm third-person framing.
- Ship centred in the lower-middle rather than filling the frame.
- Negative space around the ship.
- Mining station / industrial structure as environmental scale reference.
- One clear threat, not a swarm of UI warnings.
- Teal navigation markers and red threat markers.
- Objective and ship status readable at a glance.

## UI presentation

- Thin technical frames.
- Mostly transparent panels.
- Few large visual blocks.
- Small labels rather than giant gamey banners.
- Teal = known / useful information.
- Amber = player / action / mining.
- Red = threat.
- White = core readability.

## People / crew

The current visual canon does **not** make crew portraits or crew panels part of the baseline gameplay presentation. Avoid making crew a visible brand pillar in hero gameplay compositions unless the game later establishes a dedicated crew system.

## Reference-sheet treatment

Ship sheets should show:

- perspective hero render
- rear three-quarter view
- top / side / front outlines
- alternate loadouts
- component close-ups
- cargo / mining configurations
- physical markings
- material details

The technical sheet should clarify the ship's structure instead of inventing extra lore.

---

# 26. Brand shorthand

When briefing a new concept artist, UI artist or environment artist, the one-line direction is:

> **WAYWARD is industrial cyberpunk in a dying frontier: a stolen ore hauler, patched together with old technology, moving through a world of surveillance, scarcity and pursuit.**

And the visual shorthand is:

**LONGER SHIPS. LESS HUD NOISE. MORE INDUSTRIAL DETAIL. RESTRAINED NEON. AMBER FOR WAYWARD. TEAL FOR INFORMATION. RED FOR THE CONTINUANCE.**

That is the current visual identity to build from.
