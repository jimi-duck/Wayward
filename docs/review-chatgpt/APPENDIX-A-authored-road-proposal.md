# WAYWARD: An Authored Road

Oct 25, 2026 · @Jimi Duck

## The pitch

The archive should choose the route, not the player. Ada Aster left more than a file in the Wayward: she left a road. Her dead-man routine knows the eight old lane relays, and each one it reaches opens one more of her notes and gives up the next leg. The player follows **the Warden's Road** from Cerro to the Threshold, one fixed leg per region, and every gate becomes a story arrival instead of a place you pass through.

This gives the story a spine it has never had. Today the chart deals a different route each time, so the story has to scatter its beats wherever the player happens to land. With a fixed road, each region can be written as a chapter, with a beginning, a middle and an arrival. The jobs stop being one-off errands and join four running storylines that grow, cross and pay off at fixed points.

**What changes**

- The jump chart's free lane choice goes. A **Course** screen takes its place. It shows the leg ahead, the next stop, the gate and what is waiting there. The road is linear, with no detours.
- Each region becomes a written leg of eight stops. **Every stop must earn its existence:** a story beat, a meaningful job, a consequence, a resource decision, a person, or travel pressure that matters. A system that offers none of these is not a stop.
- Each of the eight gates gets an arrival set-piece. There, the archive opens one of Ada's notes, someone answers, and the next leg appears.
- The 25 jobs are grouped into four threads. Each thread has a recurring contact, an order and a payoff.
- Choice moves from *where to go* to *how to get there*: how long to stay, which jobs to take, whom to trust and how to get through a checkpoint.

**What stays**

The opening (Act I through Sam's code, the rename and the archive), every character and beat, all 25 jobs and their goals, the eight fragments and the Wall, Calloway's hunt, the Net, the economy, upgrades, the six endings, Arcade and the three difficulties. Nothing here removes a mechanic. It changes the order things arrive in and gives them a reason. Existing job objectives, mechanics, rewards and failure states stay unchanged. The proposal changes only their placement, their framing and how they relate to other jobs.

## The spine

The opening works because it is authored. It has one place, one goal at a time, a cost you can see (the pod torn away), a voice you trust (Sam) and a single action that changes who you are ("Input the code"). After the first jump that craft stops. The story becomes cards dealt into whichever systems the chart happened to give you. People appear once on a pop-up and are gone, and gates are places you pass through. Every region below follows the opening's rules: a goal on screen, a physical moment the player flies through, one person who returns, and an arrival that changes the question.

**The engine: the truth is a beacon.** Calloway is already hunting the Wayward from the Cerro breach onward; that is the existing pursuit. What a relay adds is knowledge. Each Warden relay that opens Ada's road does two things on its way: it tells the Offices who is carrying what (Juno, and Ada's archive), and it shows them where she is going next, because a lit relay is a point on the old Warden route. Learning more always costs more. That one rule makes the pressure rise as the story deepens. It needs no new system: in the hunt it is one rule, that Calloway stops losing time to guessing which lane the Wayward took, and the existing Net, notices and trail carry the rest.

### Why only this road

The player must feel *this is the only road Ada left me*, never *the designer won't let me go there*. Three facts make that true, and the game states each one on screen rather than in a document:

1. **In the Reach, every other lane belongs to the Net.** The Course screen draws them, dark, with the reason: *Netted · Custody holds this lane*. Ada's relays are the ones the Net stopped watching seventy years ago.
2. **Past the Narrows, nothing else is charted.** The Continuance's charts end at the old border, so other lanes show as *Uncharted · not in the archive*. Sam says it once, after the first jump: *"Those old lanes go somewhere. Nobody's charted them since the Severance. Your gran did."*
3. **The file only opens on the road.** The routine wakes only at a Warden relay. Leaving the road would mean leaving the truth behind, and Juno has nothing else.

The world beyond stays visible on the Course screen, so it reads as a real place the player cannot yet reach, not as a wall.

### Act I · The Breakout (Cerro) · unchanged

Act I runs from Contract 08-441 through the raiders, the breach, Sam's code, the rename and the archive. The archive's last line becomes the first leg's goal: *the relay at Halden's Reach still answers*. This settles today's open issue, where Halden's Reach has no fixed place on the chart.

### 1 · The Reach: *The Signed Order*

**Leg goal:** reach the Narrows. **Question:** who remembers Ada, and who kept the records?

Halden's Reach is the second stop and a real place: a Kellern port with a hospice ring. (The first, Northfold, is a quiet landfall; the Course screen shows both from the start, so the player always knows Halden's Reach comes next.) Ilse Marrow lives there, about ninety. She was a junior operator on the relay the night it went dark. After her existing card, she produces the signed closing order she has kept in a drawer for seventy years. It opens on screen as a document: its wording (power down the outer relays, take the Wardens' keys, record it as neglect) and its signature, *L. C.* The archive records it in the Wall, and only then does Ilse recognise the routine in the hull as Ada's. Juno has always carried the Aster name; her family were only ever told Ada was lost in service. If Juno cuts Ilse off, nothing is lost but Ilse: a recording from her reaches Vesta three stops later with the same order and Ada's name. Adam Brook, a company clerk, brings the ledger, whose transfers carry the same initials. Two stops later Custody takes him, and the existing rescue, divert or leave choice plays out in the open, not on a random sortie.

**Arrival, the Narrows.** The relay has been dark for seventy years. It lights when the Wayward crosses its ring, answers with Ada's Warden code, and broadcasts the handshake on every band. The first Warden's note opens: *"If the relays still know my name, so will they."* Then the Patience comes through from beyond the blockade. Calloway was already hunting a stolen hauler; now she knows it carries Ada's archive and which road it must take. Her existing hail follows (tell, lie or cut), and the player has just learned the engine.

### 2 · The Marches: *Who Stands With You*

**Leg goal:** cross the Rift. **Question:** can people cooperate when there is not enough?

Councillor Oduya's Kessel Hold needs its rigs defended and a vote carried. Wren Saffi's Drift fleet holds the manifests: "lost" ships redirected on purpose, her parents' among them.

**Arrival, the Rift.** The Rift is a torn lane and its relay is broken, so the archive cannot reach it. Someone has to talk Juno through. If Wren trusts her, Wren guides her on the radio, beacon by beacon. If Juno held Oduya's rigs, their lamps line the passage. If neither, she flies it blind through debris: harder, never blocked. The note opens on the far side. The player's choices show up as physical help in flight, not as a line of text.

### 3 · The Salvage Belt: *What Things Are Worth*

**Leg goal:** reach the Scrapgate. **Question:** who profits from wreckage?

Val offers an advance on the file, and Kesh warns that Val is selling her heading. Dov Kessler needs help cutting a recorder out of a wreck. It is Ada's black box, and Juno hears her grandmother's voice for the first time.

**Arrival, the Scrapgate.** The gate is built from the hulls of ships lost in the Severance; names are still painted on the plates. If Val's sale is still standing, the Offices are waiting here. The note on this relay is Ada's second recording, which plays only on a Warden relay: *she was ordered to keep the relays dark, and kept their keys instead.*

### 4 · The Drift Deep: *The Ring*

**Leg goal:** pass the Slow Door. **Question:** what were the lanes built to contain?

Tarik Silva's charts show the closures form a ring. The Quiet Ferry carries refugees, and an Offices agent is aboard.

**Arrival, the Slow Door.** This gate takes three minutes to open. The Ferry asks to cross with Juno, so the arrival is an escort through a field the Thren drift in, while the agent's choice (expose, protect or use) plays out. From the door the player sees the ring itself, dead relays hanging in a curve across the sky. The note reads: *"They were never keeping us in."*

### 5 · The Breaker Yards: *Who Built It*

**Leg goal:** reach the Breaker. **Question:** who built the machinery of the Severance?

Ben Anand sells the work order. Marit Voss's crews are on strike. Sam's brother works a breaker crew. Draper's two badges come out.

**Arrival, the Breaker.** The gate is one of the lane breakers itself, still standing, with the work order's serial stamped on its hull. The strike decides whether its crews let the Wayward through quietly or the yard locks down. Ada's note is a list of every breaker and who signed for it.

### 6 · The Free Ports: *Who Was in the Room*

**Leg goal:** get past the Tollgate. **Question:** who was in the room, and what did they accept?

Old Mirren names the Forty, Ines Duarte tries to buy the file, and Val's reckoning comes due.

**Arrival, the Tollgate.** Calloway's trap plays as built: the damping field, then shoot, bargain, show her father's signature, or run. This is where the Quiet Line thread pays off (see the jobs section): the people Juno moved for them come back to help her run it.

### 7 · The Hollows: *What It Was For*

**Leg goal:** open the Seam. **Question:** what are the keys, and what is the Voice obeying?

The Voice, the Thren, and Echo or Sable (never both). Juno learns that Cyrite draws the Thren.

**Arrival, the Seam.** The Voice *is* the relay. It asks for Ada's key, the one thing it was told to wait for. The implant choice comes here, because it is the first time anyone offers to read what Juno carries.

### 8 · The Last Lanes: *What You Do With It*

**Leg goal:** reach the Threshold. **Question:** what will Juno do with the truth?

Keeper Oren's three questions, Draper's fleet and Calloway's last meeting, all as built.

**Arrival, the Threshold.** The last relay holds the last note. It is the only one Ada addresses to Juno by name, because the routine has finally learned who is flying. Then the six endings.

## Progression: one road, eight legs

Each region becomes a written leg of eight stops, and each stop has a fixed role. That is 64 stops after the first system past Cerro, against about 85 today, and the road is linear. Both are decided. The principle behind the number is that **every stop must earn its existence**: if a system brings no story beat, meaningful job, consequence, resource decision, person or travel pressure, it is cut. At a rough five minutes a stop, a journey lands near six hours, though that figure is a guess until someone plays it.

### The eight stops of a leg

1. **Landfall.** The system just past the last gate. It is quiet and short. Ada's new note sets the leg's goal on screen, and the chapter save is written here.
2. **The hub.** The region's port, where its people live. The first story beat happens here, threads offer their next job, and the refit bay is open.
3. **Work.** A mining-heavy system with a thread job. This is where the purse and the tank are earned.
4. **The turn.** The second story beat, the twist that changes what the region's question means. Calloway is most likely to catch up here if the player has lingered.
5. **Breather.** A quiet system with story but no pressure. Nothing hunts and nothing runs on a timer: thread contacts call and the Wall updates as the player settles, and a player who flies through gets those lines at the next stop.
6. **Work, under pressure.** A second thread job, and the leg's hardest fight before the gate. The Net or Calloway is closest here.
7. **The reckoning.** The client's payoff (Brook, Oduya, Val, the Ferry and so on), usually at a second port.
8. **The approach.** The gate system. It has the authored checkpoint, the gate and the arrival set-piece.

The beats already written slot into these roles with very few changes. In the Reach, for example: Halden's Reach is the hub (Ilse Marrow and the order), the ledger is the turn, Brook's arrest is the reckoning, and the Narrows is the approach.

### The Course screen

The jump chart becomes a Course screen, and its question changes from "where next?" to "how far, and at what cost?" It shows:

- three layers of the road at once, so the player never confuses them: the **leg goal** (*The Narrows · an old relay, dark seventy years*), the **next stop** (*Northfold · quiet landfall*), and the **stop after** (*Halden's Reach · find the Warden's record*);
- the leg as a line of eight stops behind those, with the current one lit and the gate at the end;
- the lanes off the road, drawn dark with their reason (*Netted*, or *Uncharted*), so the world beyond is visible;
- a thread marker on each stop where a thread's next job waits;
- the pursuit in days behind, always days and never jumps, to the half day, because on one road its position is never a guess (it reads *Custody pursuit* until the Narrows, then *Governor Calloway · knows your route*);
- the Net's front behind you;
- fluid and supplies against the next jump;
- one button, **Jump to \<next stop>**.

It first appears as the Wayward flies through the Jump Gate out of the first system past Cerro, the audit dock system, before Northfold. That jump is the one that reaches Northfold, so the screen opens on the whole Reach with Northfold as the next stop.

Intel (I) and the Wall (W) stay as they are.

### Where the choice goes

Taking away lane choice does not take away choice. It moves it to decisions that carry story weight:

- **Pace.** Calloway still follows the trail day by day. On one road the only way to stay ahead is to keep moving. Every hour mining and every job is time she gains. The Course screen shows it plainly, so the cost is a choice and never a surprise.
- **Jobs.** Take them, refuse them, or settle them another way, as today. Threads make the choice weigh more, because a refusal is remembered and two threads can pull against each other.
- **People.** The existing tell, lie, rescue, leave, trust and sell choices, now arriving in a set order so their consequences can land where they are meant to.
- **Checkpoints.** How to get past each gate (see Checkpoints).

### What the code keeps

The road is data: one row per stop in a new `content/road.ts`, giving its name, kind, role, beats and thread slots. The chart becomes a single path laid from those rows. Skies, rock and features are still composed from the seed, so two journeys on the same road still look and fly differently. Calloway's trail, the Net, wrecks and adrift, chapter saves and the economy need no new rules. A wreck tows you back to the leg's last landfall or hub instead of a random system.

## Jobs that add up: four threads

All 25 jobs keep their objectives, mechanics, rewards and failure states. Only their placement, framing and relationship to other jobs change, and the contacts and meanings below are provisional (see Canon and scope). What changes is that each belongs to one of four threads. A thread has a contact who calls back, a fixed order, and a payoff at a named place on the road. Every job's card gains one line that recalls the last step, and its result line says what the step meant. Three rules hold throughout:

- **Steps come in order.** A refused step does not end the thread. The next one still arrives, and the contact remembers you said no.
- **Payoffs read the count.** Two steps or fewer earns a small payoff, three or four a partial one, five or more the full one.
- **Threads collide.** Two of the best-paid jobs hurt someone in another thread. The game says so on the card, in a chip, before you choose.

## The unknown: meeting the Thren along the road

The Thren should be met on every leg, long before anyone explains them. Today they appear mostly as late swarms in the Hollows. On the road they become the story's other presence: something at the edge of the scope that grows closer and clearer with each leg, while the human story keeps the player busy. The rules:

- **Show, never explain, until the Hollows.** No character says what they are before Act III. People have folklore, not facts.
- **One meeting per leg, rising.** It starts as a ghost on the scope, then traces, then sightings, then contact, then a swarm.
- **The tank is the tell.** Every meeting is stronger with a full tank and weaker with an empty one. The player can notice the pattern before the Survey thread confirms it, and before the Hollows names it: Cyrite draws them.
- **Not a fight until it must be.** The first four meetings cannot be won by shooting. They pass, watch or follow. Firing on them does nothing, or makes it worse.
- **The Voice is the other unknown.** It speaks rarely and always knows more than it should.

## Checkpoints, recaps and goals on screen

A fixed road lets every checkpoint have a face and every return to the game start with a reminder.

### One inspector, eight gates

Each approach stop has the Net's authored checkpoint, with the same answers as today: show your registry (with Sam's codes), spoof it, talk through, or run. Inspector Ashgrove of the Offices, already in the cast as captain of the Kind Regard, is the officer on the other end at every one (provisional). He remembers. The trick that worked at the Narrows is a long shot at the Rift, and the card says why (*"Ashgrove has seen that registry before."*). That turns a repeated card into a duel of habits, and gives the player a reason to vary answers they would otherwise repeat. His arc is short: by the Free Ports he has been demoted for missing her, and in the Last Lanes he flies with Draper's fleet, or, if Juno never once fired on his pickets, he signals her through.

The Net still spreads behind her day by day, so lingering still brings blockades into systems she has not reached yet.

### Chapters and recaps

Every landfall writes the chapter save, as gates do today. **Continue** opens on a short recap card in the archive's message style: one paragraph on where Juno is, what she carries, whom she helped or left, and the leg's question. It is written from flags, not invented. A player back after a week starts with the story in hand.

### Goals always on screen

The objective names the next stop and its purpose, with the leg's goal on a quiet line under it: Next: Halden's Reach · find the Warden's record, then The Narrows · 7 jumps*.* Each Warden's note ends with a one-line lead, and that line becomes the objective, so nobody has to read a note to know where to go. The Wall gains a **Threads** column, one line per thread: steps done, the contact, and the next stop where it calls.

### When story lines play

A fixed road only works if its beats survive the way people actually fly. Five rules hold everywhere, and the Reach chapter script spells them out:

- **Important beats queue, they never drop.** A card, radio line or Wall entry that cannot play yet waits, across a jump if it has to.
- **Radio never covers the fight.** One voice at a time on the radio strip, never over the centre, the objective, hit feedback or combat alerts.
- **Timed lines wait for a free moment:** no hostile in range, no incoming fire, no card open. Seconds in a script are a least wait, not a stopwatch.
- **Critical clues stay in the Wall** the moment they are earned, whether or not their line has played.
- **Pursuit is counted in days,** everywhere. Encounters have their own thresholds in days (amber at 1, red at half, arrival at zero), so the measure never switches to jumps.

This matters most for the unknown, which must never appear mid-fight, and for each arrival, whose five steps play strictly in order.

### Wrecks on a fixed road

The rule is unchanged: a fee, a day lost, back to two plates. The tug now tows Juno to the leg's last landfall or hub, instead of a random system, so a wreck costs distance on the road and gives Calloway a day. A total loss restarts the leg from its landfall save.

### Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The road feels like a wall | A player who sees other lanes and cannot take them blames the design | The three reasons in *Why only this road* stay on screen: dark lanes labelled Netted or Uncharted, and Sam's line |
| Less replay | One road means a second journey visits the same places | Skies stay seeded; threads, arrivals and endings change with choices; Arcade stays the replay mode |
| Less money time | 64 stops instead of 85 means less mining and fewer upgrades | Retune `STORY_PRICE` and job pay from `pilot:upgrades`, never by eye |
| Calloway loses her teeth | Without route choice her hunch has nothing to read | Pace becomes the lever: her distance shows in days, and every hour mining and every job costs time |
| Reading load | Notes, thread lines and recaps add text | Every note ends in a one-line lead on the HUD; the gist rule for pop-ups holds |
