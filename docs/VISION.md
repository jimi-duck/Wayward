# WAYWARD

*The game's vision. Status is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).*

WAYWARD is a cyberpunk space survival game about staying ahead of a system
that wants you back.

You are not a space hero. You are a fugitive aboard a stolen ore hauler,
carrying evidence that could expose seventy years of lies. The game is about moving
through a broken frontier while deciding what to spend, who to trust, what
risks to take and how much of yourself to sacrifice to keep moving.

Combat matters, but it should rarely be the point. The ideal sortie feels
like this:

> I need Cyrite. I need to reach the next system. There is a checkpoint
> ahead. A settlement is asking for help. Calloway is getting closer. I
> could fight, but maybe there's another way.

The tension comes from having too little time, fuel, information and trust.

---

## The world

### What came in between

The **Accord** was the old free union of worlds. For centuries it spread
from system to system, connected by jump lanes and powered by **Krail**, a
rare crystalline resource found beneath the surface of a handful of worlds.
Krail fed the fabrication systems that could build almost anything, kept
cities alive and economies running. Its sister mineral, **Cyrite**, violet in
the same rock, powered the jump drives and kept ships moving. A drive does
not burn it raw: a ship's refinery cooks it down to **jump fluid**, and the
Wayward, an ore hauler, has a good one. She refines as she collects.

Then the Krail started running out.

The Accord declared an emergency government to preserve what remained: to
"maintain continuity", to "protect the common good". It was supposed to be
temporary. It wasn't. The emergency government became the **Continuance**,
and seventy years later it still governs the core worlds.

- Every citizen carries an implant that records their behaviour and assigns
  a **Civic Score**. The score decides what you can access, where you can
  travel, what work you may do and how closely you are watched.
- The **Custody** is the fleet, the police and the military.
- The **Offices of Peace** is its intelligence network: it investigates
  dissent, monitors communications and deals with people whose loyalty
  begins to slip.
- **Corporations** control almost everything else: the mines, the
  factories, the ships, the food, the jobs.

The Continuance calls this order. Everyone else calls it survival.

### The Severance

Seventy years ago the jump lanes to the outer worlds went dark. Thousands of
systems became unreachable overnight. Supply chains collapsed, ships
disappeared, millions were cut off. The Continuance blamed dwindling Krail.
The region became **the Scatter**. The worlds left
behind built their own settlements, repaired their own ships, scavenged what
they could, and waited for the lanes to come back. They never did. Until
now.

### You

You escape your home system aboard a decommissioned Continuance ore hauler.
Hidden in her systems is a sealed archive, a file marked **SEVERANCE**: proof
that the blackout was a purge. The shortages were a cover, and the isolation
was used to remove those who could challenge the Continuance and to secure its
hold on power. Seventy years of shortages, billions left behind, all
deliberate.

You don't know what happens if the file reaches the outside world. You know
what happens if the Continuance finds it first.

### The Wayward

The hauler is an old **Obedience-class**. Like every Continuance vessel she
was named for an approved virtue: *Obedience*, *Duty*, *Prosperity*,
*Loyalty*. You paint over the markings and give her your own: **WAYWARD**, the
word the Continuance uses for people who refuse to stay where they belong.

You run for the Scatter, and just as you reach the dead lanes they come back
online. For
the first time in seventy years the way out is open. The Continuance follows
you through.

## The chase

**The Net.** Continuance control spreads across the star map behind you.
Systems it reaches become checkpoints, surveillance zones and controlled
markets: Cyrite trade restricted, implant scans mandatory, local leaders
pressured, resistance gone. You can slow it (help a settlement hold out,
sabotage a checkpoint, destroy a relay, supply a resistance group, expose an
operation), and every intervention costs something.

**The hunter. Governor Idris Calloway** follows you personally. She is on the
Net, moving through the same systems, shown on the chart as a shadow a few
jumps behind. She learns from where you've been. You can feed her false
information, lay misleading trails, go dark, bribe contacts, sabotage her
supply lines, or turn and fight. She sends messages: sometimes threatening,
sometimes personal, sometimes almost reasonable. She believes the
Continuance is holding civilisation together. The question is whether she
believes it because it's true, or because she needs it to be.

---

## The Scatter

- **Holdfasts**: settlements built around whatever survived (mining
  colonies, refineries, old stations, abandoned cities). Each its own small
  nation. Some want independence, some protection; some would happily sell
  you out.
- **Drifts**: nomadic communities aboard fleets of ships, following
  resources, trade and safe routes. They know the Scatter better than anyone,
  and are very hard to control.
- **The Gutter**: pirates, scavengers and abandoned crews beyond almost every
  law. Ruthless, desperate, or past caring. They raid for Cyrite and
  supplies. Most people avoid them. Some become them.
- **The Voice**: the old fabrication systems, left running with nobody to
  instruct them, began making their own decisions. Something emerged in the
  network, one intelligence or several. It speaks through terminals,
  machines, abandoned factories and things never meant to speak. It may be
  helping. It may be waiting.
- **The Thren**: nobody knows where they came from. They inhabit the old
  lanes and appear where large amounts of Cyrite are moving: vast coordinated
  swarms in which ships vanish, signals distort and navigation fails. A
  species, an organism, or something much larger. People call them the Pale,
  the Swarm, the Thren. Nobody knows what they call themselves.

The first systems should feel recognisably human. The deeper you travel, the
further the world drifts from what you understand. The Voice and the Thren
are what push the game beyond "space pirates versus evil government".

## Survival

Cyrite is everything, and it has two lives. The ship refines it into jump
fluid first, until the drive tank holds a jump; every jump burns fluid. What
the tank cannot take stays Cyrite, the money for upgrades, ports and favours.
Every mistake costs both. You also keep yourself alive: supplies, cargo,
hull. Sometimes you have enough. Usually you don't.

Every contact is a choice: a trader's deal, a settlement asking for help, a
stranger with information, a toll, a distress call, a checkpoint at the next
jump, a ship that vanishes from sensors, someone who claims to know where
Calloway is. You never have enough to deal with everything, so you decide
what matters.

### The ship: four systems

The Wayward starts slow, ugly, underarmed and barely worth stealing. You turn
her into something else:

- **Guns**: weapons, armour, direct combat. Harder to kill, or very good at
  killing first.
- **Rig**: mining, cargo, fuel efficiency, extraction. Carry more, mine
  faster, waste less Cyrite, stay out longer.
- **Signals**: sensors, hacking, spoofing, communications. Hide who you are,
  manipulate checkpoints, find things first, make the map lie.
- **Contacts**: trade, smuggling, faction relationships, people. Safer
  routes, better prices, favours, people willing to take risks for you.

There is no perfect build. You are deciding what kind of ship she becomes.

## The file

Eventually the chase stops being about escape, and you decide what the
Severance file is worth:

- **Broadcast**: release it to the core worlds and hope the truth is enough.
- **Unite**: bring the Holdfasts, Drifts and others together into something
  that can resist.
- **Sell**: to whoever offers most; become rich enough to disappear.
- **Trade**: to the Continuance, for amnesty, a new identity, maybe a way
  home.
- **Seal**: destroy the lanes again. The Scatter stays free; the core stays
  in the dark.
- **Vanish**: take the Wayward beyond the known routes and let everyone else
  decide.

Endings reflect earlier choices, relationships and reputation, not just a
final dialogue option.

---

## The Breakout

The story opens with the escape from your home system: get through the first
checkpoints, mine Cyrite, fight your way out, keep the ship alive, reach the
Scatter. A card sets the scene, a short training run follows (mine a violet
rock, fit torpedoes at the work bay, shoot one ship), and the same loop also
plays as the Arcade mode. Before you can decide what to do with the truth, you
have to survive long enough to carry it somewhere.

The story is about 85 stops in 8 regions, roughly seven hours.

## Pillars

1. **Keep moving.** The Continuance is always advancing. The map is not a
   level select; it is a pressure system.
2. **Every contact is a gamble.** Trade, jobs, warnings, pleas, tolls,
   ambushes and strangers, all with uncertain consequences.
3. **Build your own way out.** Guns, Rig, Signals, Contacts: different
   investments, meaningfully different ways of surviving.
4. **People matter.** You fly alone, but the people you meet are not stat
   bonuses: who you help and who you leave behind follows you.
5. **The frontier is alive.** Unpredictable, strange, occasionally hostile;
   not a collection of missions.

## The central test

**How far will you go to keep moving?** Run. Hide. Trade. Lie. Fight. Help
someone. Leave someone behind. Trust the wrong person. Carry the truth, or
bury it.

WAYWARD is the story of what happens in between.
