# Story direction (2026-11-14)

The owner's direction for the next pass of the story, agreed on 14 November
2026. Where this file and [STORY_BIBLE.md](STORY_BIBLE.md) or
[CLAUDE_STORY_REWRITE_BRIEF.md](CLAUDE_STORY_REWRITE_BRIEF.md) differ, this
file wins. The plan it comes from: "WAYWARD: Story direction plan"
(claude.ai/artifact/R3SfXJMxbCnJts4jBZ8Uiz).

## 1. Three themes, woven through

**Genocide, authoritarianism and the pursuit of freedom** are the story's
themes. Every leg carries all three at once, through things the player sees
and does. None of them is a topic one stop explains and the next forgets.

Under all three sits a **rigid two-class system, the Charter**.

A writing test: if a stop carries one of the themes, it also touches at least
one of the others.

## 2. The two classes: Chartered and Bonded

**The Charter is the right of passage.** A Chartered person may:

- cross any Jump Gate on their own word;
- buy jump fluid at the posted price;
- dock at any port;
- own a ship and the gear they work with;
- be treated in core clinics;
- give evidence a court will hear.

Everyone else is **Bonded**. The official term is *contract resident*. The
Bonded are tied to a contract site. They cross a gate only on an employer's
pass, for a stated purpose. Their fluid is rationed through the employer and
deducted from pay. Their gear is always leased. Their word is worth nothing
against a Chartered one.

Juno starts Bonded. WAYWARD is what the Continuance calls Bonded people who
will not stay where they are put.

**No path up.** Charters are hereditary. The public line is the *Merit Grant*:
"Charters are awarded for exceptional contribution." The Marlow allocation
tables show no Merit Grant to a Bonded worker in forty years. Every Charter
since went to board families or was bought.

Charters only move down. A Chartered person who speaks up, defaults or is
"lost in service" has their family's Charter withdrawn. The Asters lost theirs
when Ada vanished; the debt transfer clause did the rest.

**Power.**

- The Chartered minority are the only people whose consent matters. The threat
  of losing a Charter keeps them obedient. Brook, Calloway and Thane are all
  Chartered, and all have something to lose.
- The broadcasts address "Chartered citizens" and talk *about* contract
  residents.
- **The Severance was a Charter withdrawal.** One order withdrew the Charter of
  every person in the Scatter. A people with no right of passage could be left
  behind legally. The twenty-three ships turned away were Bonded ships refused
  passage. The Hollows show that the Severance order is a Charter revocation
  list.
- It still runs. Nobody in the Scatter has a Charter. Kellern buys their Cyrite
  through Bonded contracts at Bonded rates.

**Shown in play, never as a number.**

- **Gates.** Chartered traffic flies straight through. Bonded haulers wait in a
  work lane.
- **Pumps.** Core depots have two prices, and the Bonded rate is higher.
  Scatter depots have one.
- **Berths.** Core berths say "Docking denied · Charter required".
- **Pay.** Bonded job pay shows a lease deduction.
- **People.** The Northfold mother with a sick child; a kind Chartered dock
  officer who would help if it did not cost his Charter; a Chartered dock
  worker who thinks the ring keeps her children safe.

**Choices.**

- **A forged Charter** (Val in the Belt, the Guild in the Free Ports) opens
  gates and berths and makes fluid cheaper. A scan can strip it, and heat
  jumps. It is never a lockout either way, but the Sell and Trade endings
  remember it.
- **Carrying the Bonded through gates** (the Quiet Line) costs time, fluid and
  heat, and brings people to the Threshold.
- **At Thane's hearing** Bonded evidence is "not admissible". Juno needs a
  Chartered voice: Brook, Calloway, or Lucan's own signature.

**Rule.** The Charter is yes or no. It is never a score, a bar or a number to
grind. The Civic Score stays removed.

## 3. The central revelation

There was a cheaper way to stop the Thren. The Continuance chose to sacrifice
the Scatter instead, and it still does.

1. **The danger was real, and mining made it.** Thren pressure rose with the
   extraction rate. Ada's readings showed it.
2. **The fix was on the table.** Lucan Calloway's "ten years and a public
   warning" meant cutting Cyrite extraction for a decade and evacuating the
   outer worlds. The board refused, because it would crash Krail.
3. **So they built a fence and put people on the wrong side of it.** The
   breaker ring holds the Thren in the Hollows, with the Scatter as the
   buffer. The Krail "shortage" story kept anyone from asking.
4. **The rescue was stopped on purpose.** The evacuation ships were built, then
   broken up at Yard Nine for breaker parts.
5. **It is still running.** Kellern's contracts keep the Scatter mining at full
   rate inside the ring, so the Thren press on the Scatter. The Survey buries
   its reports. Settlements that cost too much to supply are left to fail.

**Levels of guilt.** Most of the Forty believed the buffer was a terrible
necessity. Lucan argued against it and signed anyway.

A small **inner circle** made sure the Scatter could never come back:

- Kellern's chair;
- the Offices' director of that day;
- the Survey's chief.

Calloway inherited the necessity story and believes it. Draper is today's
profit.

**Genocide.** The inner circle knew the abandonment would kill most of the
outer worlds and planned for it. Kellern's *attrition schedule* priced which
settlements would fail, and when. That is intent. The game shows it plainly,
through one document and its people. The Drift call it "the Turning Away";
Old Mirren says the word.

## 4. Fixed canon and open canon

**Fixed.**

- Juno flies alone.
- Krail, Cyrite and jump fluid stay distinct.
- The six endings: Broadcast, Unite, Sell, Trade, Seal and Vanish.
- The eight legs and their stops.
- Ada is Juno's grandmother.
- The Thren are real, strange and never translated.
- Lucan Calloway signed the order.
- The breach debt is 800 CR.
- No Civic Score.

**Decided 14 November.**

- **The Severance was abandonment as policy**, with an inner circle that made
  sure it could not be undone (§3).
- **The keys work in any hand.** There is no bloodline lock and Juno is not a
  chosen one. Ada's routine only nudged her onto the hull. Note 8 admits it,
  and Juno resents it.
- **Unite is a confederation with no governor:** councils, crews and fleets
  with shared defence. It is slow and quarrelsome, and nobody can promise it
  holds.

**Open** (write freely, within the above):

- scene-level dialogue and answer labels;
- the broadcasts;
- side characters' lives;
- the Thren's ecology beyond "mining draws them";
- the epilogue lines.

## 6. The Continuance's voice

**Continuance Public Service** is a radio source. Its broadcasts are short,
official, flat and plausible.

- **When:** about one per stop at most, on arrival in a held system. Never in a
  breather, and never over a fight.
- **What:** each tells a lie the player will later disprove. It is addressed to
  Chartered citizens. As Juno acts, the broadcasts change to talk about her.
- **Who:** the face behind them is **Commissioner Aurel Thane, Office of Public
  Record**. Thane meets Juno in the Free Ports and holds the hearing.

| Sell | Freedom for one | Juno is paid in a real Charter and flies the Chartered lane alone. |
| Trade | Reform from inside | Merit Grants are finally issued: a ladder with a handful of rungs. |
| Seal | Nobody passes | The Charter means nothing, and neither does freedom of movement. |
| Vanish | Refuse the role | The work lane at the gate is still there in the last shot. |

## 8. Traps

- **No cartoon empire.** The broadcasts sound reasonable, and ordinary people
  defend the system.
- **No perfect rebels.**
- **The Thren stay strange.** No boss fight.
- **Juno is the catalyst, not the saviour.** The epilogues credit the people.
- **No wall of new cards.** One voice at a time and the caps in
  `tests/caps.test.ts` still hold.
