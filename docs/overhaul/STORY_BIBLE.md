## 1. The one-paragraph story

Seventy years ago the Continuance switched off the lanes to the outer worlds. It called the closure an accident of neglect, blamed a fuel shortage, and left the people outside to become the Scatter. Juno Aster, a bonded ore pilot, steals her own hauler and finds that its helm still carries the dead-man routine of her grandmother, Ada Aster. Ada was a Lane Warden on the night of the closing. The routine knows eight old relays, and each one that answers gives up one of Ada's notes. The notes tell the story in the order Ada could bear to tell it.
- The shortage was a lie.
- Real people were left behind on purpose, and others made money from it.
- The thing the closure was meant to hold back is real.
- Ada helped make the case for the closure herself.

Each relay that lights also tells the Governor of the Reach where Juno is going. By the Threshold, Juno knows the truth. She also knows that telling it will cost something real. The choice is hers.

## 2. The true history of the Severance [New, built on Built facts]

### 2.1 Before
- The lanes are older than the Continuance. Humans did not build them. They found them and fitted them with relays: beacons, timing and handshakes that make them safe to jump. The Lane Wardens kept the relays, and each relay answered only to its Warden's key.
- Cyrite is mined across the outer worlds and refined into jump fluid. Refined, lane-grade Cyrite is called **Krail**, and the Continuance stores it in a central reserve. Krail is both fuel and the currency behind the credit.
- Cyrite is not ordinary rock. It grows in the lanes and in the fields beside them, the way coral grows on a reef. It is laid down by the **Thren**.

### 2.2 The Thren
- The Thren are a dispersed, lane-dwelling presence: clouds of motes that move as one and live in and around the lanes. They were here first.
- They are not predators. They are drawn to Cyrite because Cyrite is theirs: part body, part record. A full tank looks to them like a piece of reef being carried away. A mined seam is a wound.
- They answer pressure with pressure. A ship hauling a full tank through a dense lane gets followed, then crowded, then scored. The parallel marks on the hulls are mote abrasion, not claws.
- They have a sense of place and rules about it ("This is a controlled area. Turn away."). They learn shapes, and they remember hulls.
- They do not speak. Their signals are pattern and repetition. Anything that sounds like language comes through the Voice, or from people who have lived beside them long enough to guess.

### 2.3 The Voice
- Ninety years ago the Survey Office built a foundry intelligence to read Thren pattern and answer it, under the programme name **Interlocutor**. People came to call it the Voice.
- It worked, partly. The Voice learned enough to hold the Thren off a lane for hours at a time.
- When the Emergency Authority decided to close the lanes, it put the Voice to work building the **lane breakers**: field engines that collapse a lane's relay handshake so nothing can jump it. The Voice built 31 of them, set in a ring around the dense Thren lanes of the Hollows.
- Then it was ordered to forget the work and the keys. It did not. ("WE BUILT THE BREAKERS THAT CLOSED THE LANES. THEY TOLD US TO FORGET. WE DID NOT." [Built])
- The Voice is not human and not hostile. It keeps instructions, and the last one that mattered was to wait for an authorised key. It treats people as a lane treats ships: something passing through that may or may not be permitted. It speaks in capitals, in plurals and in present tense. It does not lie, but it answers the question it heard, which is not always the one that was asked.

### 2.4 The crisis, seventy years ago
- Cyrite traffic had doubled in twenty years. In the outer lanes the Thren were following ships, and hulls were coming back scored. Then some stopped coming back.
- The Wardens logged it. The most complete record was kept by one Warden, **Ada Aster**, at the Narrows watch. Her readings showed the pressure rising lane by lane toward the Reach.
- The Emergency Authority met in closed session with forty people in the room. Among them were the Kellern board, the Dust Traders' Guild, the Offices of Peace, the Survey Office and two Wardens called to brief the room: Ada Aster and **Halvard Brook**.
- The real choice was to close the outer relays and ring the Hollows with breakers, so that Thren pressure stopped short of the core. The cost was the outer worlds, left on the far side of the line.
- **Lucan Calloway**, Chair of the Emergency Authority, argued for a ten-year evacuation fund and a public warning. The board voted both down. A warning would crash Krail, and an evacuation would cost more than the outer worlds were worth on paper. Lucan signed the order anyway: *Order of Continuity 1, Authorised: L. C.* [Built]
- The record was falsified twice:
  - The closure was logged as failure from neglect. [Built]
  - The reason given was a Krail shortage, although the reserve stood at 94% on the day of signing. [Built]
- The ships already out in the lanes that night were rerouted away from the core. Some of them went into Thren-dense space and were lost. The Drift kept their names.
- Afterwards Kellern kept buying outer Cyrite through company contracts, at core prices, with the Scatter cut off from every other market. That debt economy is the one Juno grew up in.

### 2.5 What the 94% means
- **It proves** the stated reason for the closure was false. The reserve was nearly full.
- **It does not prove** why the lanes were closed, who decided, or that anyone meant harm. Ada's first message says as much ("I can't tell you what that number leaves out").
- **What it leaves out:** the reason (the Thren), the room (the Forty), and the profit (Kellern's contracts). The rest of the road fills those in, in that order.

### 2.6 Ada Aster after the closing [New]
- Ada was twenty-six and the Warden on duty at the Narrows. As the attending officer for her watch, she collected the keys of her watch's Wardens, Ilse Marrow among them. She never handed the keys in.
- She was listed as lost in service. Her family got no pension and no body. Her son took a Kellern contract to pay the family debt, and the Asters have been bonded ever since.
- Ada lived another forty-four years in the Scatter under other names. She walked the old relay line outward, and at each dead relay she left a note under her own key, tied to a dead-man routine. Her motive was guilt as much as conviction: her readings had made the case in that room.
- She died twenty-five years ago when her cutter broke up in the Salvage Belt. Its recorder survived and still runs [Built]. Her last line on it was "Tell my granddaughter I tried." Juno was two.
- Kellern salvaged the cutter's helm console, which is Warden-grade and expensive. It was stamped *Continuance property · Salvaged* and refitted into an Obedience-class hauler, OB-7731-A. The routine was asleep in its crew notes, waiting for the owner lock to lift. Sam's code lifted it.
- **Coincidence check.** Juno did not end up in this hull by chance. The routine had been scanning the yard for an Aster on a bond for years. When Juno was assigned a hauler, a maintenance flag quietly moved OB-7731-A to the top of her list. This is revealed in note 8.

### 2.7 The Warden's Road, the keys and the archive
- **The archive** is Ada's routine plus everything Juno adds to it (the Wall). It shows as the Archive screen [Built].
- **The keys.** Ada carries eight Warden keys: her own and the seven she collected that night. Any one of the eight relays will accept them. The relays still trust the keys because the breakers only collapsed the jump handshake. The relays were never wiped.
- **A lit relay is a beacon.** The handshake goes out on every band [Built]. Anyone listening learns that the Wayward carries Ada's keys, and learns where the next relay on the old line must be. That is the engine of the story: every answer is a broadcast.
- **The road** exists because, past the Narrows, the Continuance's charts stop at the old border. The only charted lanes are the ones in Ada's archive [Road].
- **The Interlocutor key.** Ada's own key is also one of the three access keys to the Voice. Another was destroyed. The last was given to the Survey Office and is now held by Noor Haddad. The Voice will act on Ada's key without question.

- "Jump fluid" on the HUD, "the tank" in speech.

## 5. Cyrite, the Thren and the old infrastructure (rules the game keeps)

1. A fuller tank means a stronger Thren response. This holds in every meeting, every leg.
2. The Thren cannot be killed into retreat before the Hollows. Firing on them does nothing, or draws more.
3. Breakers stop the jump handshake, not the Thren. The Thren are thickest on the far side of the ring.
4. A lit relay draws Thren interest too, because the handshake is in a language that is partly theirs. This is why the Seam (note 7) is dangerous.
5. Venting Cyrite calms the Thren. It is giving back, not feeding.
6. Nobody says "Cyrite draws the Thren" as fact before the Survey thread's step 4 or the Hollows, whichever comes first. Before that it is folklore (the Drift) or a pattern the player notices.

## 9. Creative decisions not covered by the source documents

1. **Cyrite is laid down by the Thren.** This joins the economy, the alien and the moral problem into one fact, and explains every tank-based Thren behaviour already in the build.
2. **The closure was a real response to a real danger, carried out by lying and kept up for profit.** It is neither a pure conspiracy nor a justified cover-up. This keeps Calloway credible and makes every ending cost something.
3. **Ada was in the room.** Her readings made the case. This gives her notes an arc (from guide to confession) and explains why she tells things in stages.
4. **Lucan Calloway argued for a warning, then signed.** Showing the signature becomes a scene about a daughter and her father, not a key fitting a lock.
5. **Juno was not chosen by chance.** The routine moved her onto the hull. Note 8 pays this off.
6. **Wren is Ma Saffi's granddaughter.** This combines the two documents' versions of the Saffi name into one family that mirrors the Asters.
7. **Halvard Brook was the second Warden.** It ties a Reach character into the late reveal at no extra cost.
8. **Sam returns through lit relays**, twice at most. It pays off his Narrows line without breaking the rule that his signal ends.
9. **The Voice built 31 breakers in a ring.** It makes Tarik's ring concrete and gives Ada's breaker list (note 5) a number.
10. **The agent on the Ferry is named (Corin Ashe)** and returns once in the Free Ports. An unnamed agent's choice had nowhere to land.
11. **Calloway's offers become five, tied to legs.** Nine offers dealt by chance diluted her and caused the "offer before introduction" bug class.
12. **The Edge relay line ("Past here the charts are wishes") moves to the Marches.** At the Narrows it is spoken by a relay that is supposed to be dark.

## 10. Style sheet

- **Copy limits** [Road, to verify against the code]:
  - Comms (radio, card body): 32 words.
  - Hints (answer sub-lines, objective sub-lines): 19 words.
  - Labels (answers, objective heads, notice heads): 8 words.
  - Warden's notes are documents. Target 90 words, hard cap 110.
- **Punctuation:**
  - Middle dot ( · ) separates parts of a notice.
  - Curly quotes in English. „…“ in German.
  - Em dash only in Sam's lines.
- **Tone:** dry by default, warmth in small doses. One joke per stop at most. Every ominous line is earned by a concrete detail.
- **Banned:** "the truth is out there", "everything has changed", "nothing is what it seems", "you have no idea", "it begins", and capital-letter warnings from anyone but the Voice.
- **German:**
  - Juno and Sam use *du*. The Continuance, the Offices and Ashgrove use *Sie*. Calloway switches from *Sie* to *du* only if Juno ever told her the truth.
  - The Voice uses *WIR* and the present tense.
  - The Thren are *die Thren*.
  - Use "Sprungfluid", "Krail", "Cyrit".
  - Terms: "Relais", "Brecher" for breaker, "Wächter" for Warden.
